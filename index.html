What is Total Factor Productivity (TFP)?

Rather than measuring a single yield like tonnes of wheat per hectare, Total Factor Productivity (TFP) compares a farm's total combined outputs (crops, beef, wool, milk) against its total combined inputs (land, labour, machinery, fuel, and fertiliser). Indexed to a baseline of 100, a rise to 176 means farmers produce 76% more food and fibre from the exact same resource footprint.

Key Farming Terms Explained

Broadacre farming refers to large-scale commercial cropping (wheat, barley, canola) and extensive livestock grazing (beef cattle and sheep). GVAP measures the Gross Value of Agricultural Production at wholesale market prices ($B AUD), while Land Use Density maps the physical spatial footprint of farming enterprises across Australia's 7.7M km² continent.

From Wool to Wheat & Oilseeds

In 1985, Australia still "rode on the sheep's back," with Wool & Sheepmeat ranking #1 in national production value ($9.5B real).

Following the collapse of the Australian Wool Reserve Price Scheme in 1991, millions of hectares of sheep pasture were converted into mixed cropping paddocks.

Oilseeds & Canola climbed steadily from #6 to #4 ($13.8B) as breeders introduced high-yielding hybrid canola varieties that act as a profitable root-disease break for wheat rotations.
