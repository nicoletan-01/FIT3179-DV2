What is Total Factor Productivity (TFP)?

Rather than measuring a single yield like tonnes of wheat per hectare, Total Factor Productivity (TFP) compares a farm's total combined outputs (crops, beef, wool, milk) against its total combined inputs (land, labour, machinery, fuel, and fertiliser). Indexed to a baseline of 100, a rise to 176 means farmers produce 76% more food and fibre from the exact same resource footprint.

Key Farming Terms Explained

Broadacre farming refers to large-scale commercial cropping (wheat, barley, canola) and extensive livestock grazing (beef cattle and sheep). GVAP measures the Gross Value of Agricultural Production at wholesale market prices ($B AUD), while the Murray-Darling Basin (MDB) spans NSW, VIC, QLD, and SA as Australia's primary irrigated food bowl.

Competing Without a Safety Net

Combining the OECD Agricultural Policy Monitoring and Evaluation (2025) and USDA International Productivity datasets reveals a striking policy contrast.

Australian farmers derive just 2.4% of their gross receipts from government subsidies (Producer Support Estimate, or PSE %)—compared to 17.8% across European Union nations, 37.6% in Japan, and 44.2% in South Korea.

Deregulation and export competition pushed Australian farms to adopt efficiency innovations early, lifting cumulative TFP to 164.2 (1990 = 100), outpacing the United States (156.8) and Canada (159.4).

From Wool to Wheat & Oilseeds

In 1985, Wool & Sheepmeat ranked #1 in national production value ($9.5B real) in Australia.

Following the collapse of the Australian Wool Reserve Price Scheme in 1991, millions of hectares of sheep pasture were converted into mixed cropping paddocks.

Oilseeds & Canola climbed steadily from #6 to #4 ($13.8B) as breeders introduced high-yielding hybrid canola varieties that act as a profitable root-disease break for wheat rotations.

Section 05 • Global Market Integration

Global Outlets of Efficiency: The International Footprint of Farm Productivity

Because Australia produces roughly three times more food and fibre than its domestic population of 27 million can consume, over 70% of total agricultural production ($75.8 billion in 2024–25) is exported to international markets. Operating under the OECD's second-lowest farm subsidy regime (Producer Support Estimate: 2.4%), Australian farmers rely entirely on continuous Total Factor Productivity growth (+1.52% p.a.) and scale to feed five key global trading corridors.
